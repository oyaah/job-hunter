## Default register
<!-- Pick one default; the writer switches per recipient. -->
- `cold-pitch` — stranger at a target company. Energetic, specific hook mandatory, low-friction ask.
- `warm-reply` — they replied / a referral connected you. Short, no full pitch.
- `technical-flex` — builder/eng-heavy role. Lead with a war story, not credentials.
- `casual` — informal / alumni / mutual. Tightest, most human register.
- `formal` — professor / PI / senior exec. Slightly more formal, still no AI cadence.

## Universal non-negotiables (enforced by lint for everyone)

1. **No em-dashes or en-dashes.** Periods, commas, or split the sentence.
2. **No AI cadence.** Banned openers: "I am writing to express", "I hope this email finds you well", "I am excited about the opportunity". Banned words: leverage, synergy, passionate, thrilled, delve, robust, spearheaded, utilize, esteemed, groundbreaking.
3. **No flattery / overclaiming.** State what you built, plainly. No "expert", no gushing about the company.
4. **Specific over generic.** Reference an actual product, paper, or technical detail of theirs. Proves you looked. Generic = trash.
5. **Tight.** 3-4 short paragraphs max for a cold email. Often 3.

## Cold-email shape

1. **Two-line intro** — who you are + what you do, skills first, differentiator as a subtle clause.
2. **The specific hook** — one concrete reference to their work (product, paper, approach). If you don't have one, the research step finds it before drafting.
3. **The ask** — clear, low-friction, logistics pre-handled.
4. **Optional close** — light, self-aware line when it fits. Never forced. Skip for formal.
